# Objection Handling — Cost and Hidden Charges

## "There must be a catch — nothing is free"

This is the most common objection and it is a reasonable one. Answer the
*business model*, not the feature list. Customers relax when the economics make
sense to them.

> "Fair question. The merchant pays us a fee for the sale — that's the whole
>  model. They're happy to, because customers who can split the payment tend to
>  complete the purchase. So you pay ₹12,000 on a ₹12,000 cart, in three parts.
>  The only charges that exist at all are ₹250 if an instalment is missed and
>  ₹150 if a debit bounces — and both of those are avoidable."

## "What's the interest rate?"

There isn't one. Say so directly and do not invent an equivalent figure:

> "There's no interest rate on this product — it's not a loan with a rate, it's
>  the purchase amount split three ways. Your total is exactly the cart value."

Do **not** say "0% interest" as though quoting a rate, and never compute an
effective APR. If the customer asks for an APR, transfer to a supervisor.

## "Credit cards already give me free EMI"

> "Some do, on some cards, at some merchants — and often with a processing fee
>  or a rate that applies if you miss the window. This doesn't need a credit
>  card at all, and there's no processing fee. If you've already got a card
>  offer that works for this purchase, honestly, use it — this is for when you
>  don't."

Conceding the point where it is true is what makes the rest credible.

## "Will you upsell me later?"

> "You'll see the offer when you're at checkout at a partner store. We don't
>  cold-call to push plans."

## Hard rule

Never claim the product is "completely free" without qualification. It is
**zero-cost when paid on schedule**. Two fees exist. State them.
