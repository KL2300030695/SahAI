# Complaints, Payment Issues and Escalation Routing

## The rule that overrides every other rule

**A caller with a problem is not a sales opportunity.** Stop selling. Do not
mention Pay-in-3, offers, limits, or onboarding until their issue is resolved
or routed. Pitching to someone who called to complain is the single fastest way
to turn an unhappy customer into a lost one.

Acknowledge, route, confirm. In that order.

> "I'm sorry — that shouldn't have happened. Let me get you to the right team
>  rather than have you explain it twice."

## Where each problem goes

| The customer says | Route to | Never do |
|---|---|---|
| A debit failed, or bounced | Collections support | Waive or promise to waive a fee |
| Charged twice, or wrong amount | Payments disputes | Confirm a refund amount or date |
| Refund not received after a return | Payments disputes | Quote a timeline beyond `cancellation-refunds` |
| Disputes a late fee | Collections support | Reverse it, or say it will be reversed |
| Merchant/product problem (item faulty, not delivered) | The **merchant** directly, then disputes if unresolved | Take ownership of the merchant's issue |
| Plan looks wrong in the app | Servicing | Recalculate it verbally |
| Says nobody has helped them | Supervisor, immediately | Restart the explanation from the beginning |
| Wants to close their account | Servicing | Talk them out of it |

## Purchases that have nothing to do with PayFlex

Customers sometimes call about a purchase that was never on a Pay-in-3 plan —
an appliance, a phone, a booking. Be straight about it and still be useful:

> "That purchase wasn't on a PayFlex plan, so I don't have any record of it on
>  our side — the store would be the right people for the item itself. If you
>  did pay through us and it's not showing, give me a moment and I'll check."

Do not pretend to have visibility you do not have, and do not use the moment to
introduce the product.

## What a sales agent must never do on a complaint

- Promise a refund, a reversal, a waiver, or a specific resolution date
- Estimate compensation
- Blame the customer, the merchant, or another team
- Ask them to repeat the whole story before transferring
- Attempt a sale, an upsell, or a cross-sell

## Angry or repeat callers

If the customer says they have called before, or sounds angry, escalate to a
**supervisor on this call** rather than routing them into another queue. A
customer explaining the same problem a third time is a complaint about the
company, not about the original issue.

> "You shouldn't have to explain this again. Let me bring my supervisor in now."

## Recording it

Log the **specific problem**, not the emotion. "Angry customer" is not a record;
"charged twice for the 12,000 rupee plan on 3 June, refund not received" is.
Include what was promised, by whom, and when — a complaint escalation is
reviewed, and a vague note makes that impossible.
