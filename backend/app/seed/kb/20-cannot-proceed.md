# When the Customer Cannot Proceed

Every other document in this handbook describes a customer who *can* onboard.
This one covers the cases where they cannot, because those are the calls agents
handle worst.

The knowledge base previously had nothing for "I don't have an Aadhaar." Asked
that, the assistant retrieved the nearest Aadhaar content — the privacy
objection, "I don't want to *share* my Aadhaar" — and answered a question the
customer had not asked, reassuring them about OTPs on an Aadhaar-linked number
they do not have. Topical similarity is not the same as answering the question,
and the gap has to be filled with the honest answer rather than papered over.

## No Aadhaar

**Aadhaar is mandatory.** Pay-in-3 onboarding uses Aadhaar OTP e-KYC and there
is no alternative route today. A customer without an Aadhaar number cannot
complete KYC and cannot be onboarded.

> "I have to be straight with you — Aadhaar is required for the verification
>  step, and there isn't an alternative on our side right now. So I can't get
>  you set up today. If you do get one later, the whole process takes about two
>  minutes and I'd be glad to help you through it."

Do not suggest a workaround, do not offer to "check with the team", and do not
imply an exception may be possible. Record the outcome as a drop-off with the
reason **no Aadhaar**, so the product team sees how often this happens.

## No PAN

PAN is also mandatory and there is no alternative. Same treatment: say so
plainly, close warmly, log the reason.

## Outside the age range

Eligibility is 21 to 60 years. A customer outside it cannot be onboarded, and
this is not negotiable at the agent's discretion.

## Not an Indian resident

Pay-in-3 is available to Indian residents only.

## The principle

A clear no, given early, is better service than a hopeful maybe. A customer
told to "try and see" spends ten minutes on KYC, fails at the Aadhaar step, and
becomes a complaint. Naming the blocker in the first minute costs the sale that
was never available and keeps the relationship.

Never invent an exception, an escalation path, or a "let me check" that does not
exist. If a customer cannot proceed, the agent's job is to say so kindly and
close.
